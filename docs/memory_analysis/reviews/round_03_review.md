**There are two distinguishable findings.** The first asks what recommendation contributes after a useful memory association has already been accepted. Recognition, graph connections, initial scores, fusion, and source budget are fixed. The observed effect is on evidence outside the accepted facts' own sources: complete annotated support rises from 635 to 689 questions, with 56 gains and two losses. The film example is particularly informative because both directors are already recognized, and changing one existing biography connection removes or restores the missing support. This makes the result more specific than a graph-versus-embedding ablation: the remaining failure concerns which property-bearing source accompanies an already matched association.

The second asks whether presenting a corrected fact is enough when recommendation still follows the old association. The FC-MH intervention changes the accepted authorship while keeping the correction in both final reader inputs. The London source disappears and all four readers fail; restoring the accepted fact restores the source and correct answers. This isolates a different failure from the first finding. The first holds accepted facts fixed and varies how their evidence connections are used; the second holds the available correction fixed and varies which association controls the subsequent evidence choice. A system can fail either test independently. The section therefore contains two related causal questions, rather than merely giving new names to two module ablations.

The novelty is empirical and method-specific. CatRAG already motivates complete evidence chains and explicitly distinguishes key-fact passage enhancement from query-dependent graph steering; its example also requires identifying a second source through an intermediate entity. The broad slogan that matched information can point to further evidence is consequently not new. The present addition is the matched AMOR control, the direct-source ceiling, the source-level decomposition, and the reversible edge intervention. The transferred released operator gives useful concrete context: 63.7% on unit weights versus 68.9% for AMOR and 69.4% in combination. It does not establish that every possible recognized-fact boost is inferior. The protocol correctly records differences in directionality, magnitude, and normalization, and the main text restricts its claim to the concrete mechanisms. [CatRAG primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01965v1).

Likewise, the requirement that edits affect downstream answers is already central to MQuAKE and its MeLLo method. The FC result is useful because it localizes a failure within this system: the correction is visible to the reader, but the selected supporting source is wrong or absent. The text cites MQuAKE and presents the new result as a specific intervention, so I do not read it as claiming discovery of edit propagation itself. [MQuAKE primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.971/).

The main limits are real but do not invalidate the present claims. The 1,259/1,260 direct-support ceiling makes the exclusivity of gains outside matched sources unsurprising; its value is locating the residual contribution, not demonstrating an unexpected universal law. The FC case establishes existence and a local mechanism, not the prevalence of that mechanism across all 100 questions. The population recognition control changes candidate facts more broadly, and its small EM reductions cannot be counted as 100 replications of the authorship intervention. The manuscript does not make that inference. The fixed-weight answer improvements are modest, and the larger full-system differences cannot be assigned wholly to weighting; the revised main source explicitly separates these comparisons and reports the pruning control. The seed-42 development evaluation is disclosed, so these findings support an explanation of this evaluated method rather than a held-out generalization claim.

Coverage meets the requested scope. Quantitative results retain all six settings and 3,386 configured questions, explicitly identify the 1,000-question 2Wiki subset, distinguish three benchmark suites from four data origins, and preserve native answer metrics. The four-origin example table combines evidence of different strengths without pretending they are identical interventions: 2Wiki is a support-selection intervention, FC is a fact intervention, RULER is a system comparison, and LoCoMo is a presentation intervention. Four readers share selections, so they demonstrate downstream consistency rather than four independent selection experiments.

The LoCoMo boundary is important to the credibility of the recommendation-centered argument. Two negative recommendation effects remain in Table 1, and the neighbor control shows a sizeable benefit on 841 single-hop questions alongside a sizeable refusal cost on 446 adversarial questions. Preserving surrounding conversational context is already motivated by AnchorMem; the new value here is the observed benefit/harm tradeoff under fixed centers, not the general observation that isolated utterances lose context. This is appropriately kept as a boundary rather than inflated into a third recommendation discovery. [AnchorMem primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17377v1).

The figure and table are informative and legible at the intended page scale. The type comparison uses official categories and shows that identical support counts do not imply identical recommendation gains. The intervention panel makes correction presence explicit and does not draw an unsupported traversal path.
